This fifteen-minute Bitcoin bet just jumped from fifty cents to eighty. The first buyers were watching another screen.
Up wins if Bitcoin ends at or above where it started. One Up plus one Down is backed by one dollar, and the winner takes it. So sixty-two cents means a sixty-two percent chance.
Each side has an order book. Bids to buy, asks to sell, and a spread between.
You bid sixty cents on Up. A stranger bids forty on Down. Nobody's selling. But sixty plus forty is a dollar, so the exchange mints a fresh pair and fills you both.
You waited in the book, so you pay nothing. The stranger filled instantly, so they pay up to three percent. And selling costs most at fifty-fifty. Limitless says that discourages flip-flopping.
Now weigh the book. Bids minus asks, over both. When bids are heavier, price tends to drift up.
That other screen? Binance, one of the fastest places Bitcoin moves show up. Traders check it against the Price to Beat and the clock, and buy when Up looks cheap.
But this market settles on Chainlink's sixty-second average. A last-second spike barely moves it, so chasing that candle can burn you.
Add fees and a half-second delay, and you can lose your whole stake. Then the close becomes the next round's Price to Beat.
